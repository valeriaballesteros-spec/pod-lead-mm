•Workada at a Glance

At Workada, everything we build, every project, every partnership, every platform decision, comes back to three things. These aren't values we put on a wall. They're the principles that shape how we work, what we reward, and where we're headed together.

Quality

Quality is our north star. We don't trade great work for speed or scale. Every annotation matters because it shapes real AI outcomes, and because it determines what contributors can access, earn, and achieve on the platform.

Quality isn't a standard we impose. It's the key that opens every door. The more your pod brings it, the more Workada brings back: more projects, more opportunity, more room to grow.

Community

Workada isn't just a platform, it's a community. We look out for our contributors, and they raise the quality of everything we build. That's not a tagline, it's how this actually works, and it's a big part of what your pod is building day to day.

When contributors grow, in skill, in earnings, in impact, Workada grows too. We invest in the people who show up, do the work, and push each other to be better.

Opportunity

At Workada, there's no ceiling on what a contributor can achieve. The opportunity to grow, as an individual, in their career, and within the company, is real and ongoing.

High-quality contributors always have projects waiting. The pipeline stays full for those who keep the bar high, show up, deliver, and the doors keep opening. That's the Workada promise, and part of your job is helping your pod get there.

About the role

Our work gets done in pods, small teams of contributors working a project together. As a Pod Lead, you are a player-coach: you do the work alongside your pod and lead it. You set the standard by example, you're the first person your contributors turn to, and you're ultimately accountable for your pod's success.

That means leading from the front on the three things that matter most, quality, hours, and retention: while building a pod that people actually want to be part of. If you like rolling up your sleeves on real tasks and turning a group of individuals into a high-performing, connected team, this role is for you.

What you'll own

  • Lead by example. Work tasks alongside your pod and set the bar for quality, throughput, and consistency. Your own output is the standard the pod measures against.
  • Be the first line of support. Serve as the first point of contact for your contributors, answer questions, resolve blockers fast, and keep the work flowing. Escalate to Ops only what truly needs it.
  • Drive quality. Review work against project guidelines, give clear and constructive feedback, and coach contributors toward stronger, more consistent output.
  • Drive hours and throughput. Keep your pod active and on pace, monitor capacity and deadlines, and make sure committed hours translate into completed work.
  • Drive retention. Spot at-risk contributors early, understand why people stay or leave, and take action to keep your best people engaged and growing.
  • Build community. Create a pod that feels supported and connected, run regular check-ins, recognize good work, and foster a culture people want to stick with.
  • Advocate for fixes. Surface recurring friction, unclear guidelines, and tooling or process problems to the ops team, and push for the changes that make your pod's work better.
  • Own pod success. Track the metrics that matter (quality scores, completion rates, active hours, retention) and take ultimate responsibility for how your pod performs.

What we're looking for

  • Experience leading or coordinating a team, group, or project, you're comfortable being the person others rely on.
  • Willingness to be hands-on: you're happy doing the work, not just overseeing it.
  • Strong communication and people skills; you can give feedback that lands and keep morale high remotely.
  • A quality- and process-oriented mindset with sharp attention to detail.
  • Comfort working with metrics to spot problems and track progress.
  • Calm, dependable problem-solving in a fast-changing, early-stage environment.
  • Strong async habits and availability that overlaps with your pod's working hours.

Workada is an equal opportunity organization. We welcome contractors of all backgrounds.

Quality Bar & Coaching

The 1–5 scale, failure modes, remediation, and how you're evaluated

What's the difference between Average Score and Dimension Score, and which should I coach off of?

Coach off Dimension Score, it's the direct per-dimension feedback. Don't weight the Average Score.

What failure modes should I be watching for?

  • Lazy responses: generic justifications that don't engage the actual content. The biggest issue, and the hardest for software to catch, sample every contributor.
  • Flipped justifications: the written comment favors one option but the score picks the other.
  • Copy-paste: the same response reused across dimensions or tasks.
  • Too many ties: more than 2 ties in a task usually means the work wasn't looked at carefully.
  • No explanation: a verdict without a justification.
  • Truncated rationales: trailing commas or dangling conjunctions, submitted mid-thought.

What does dishonest, borderline, and stellar work actually look like?

Dishonest examples: a one-word 'test' or 'see above' reused across every dimension; a written justification that names one image while the score selects the other. Borderline examples: a real observation buried in a fragment ('Mainly the barcode') with no explanation of why it matters; a sentence that ends mid-thought. Stellar examples: justifications that name multiple concrete, checkable details in one sentence and walk through a prompt's constraints item by item, crediting both options fairly.

How do I run the remediation loop?

Diagnose → re-teach → practice together → recheck.

How can I coach my pod members if I can't see any of their metrics or feedback from the MM platform?

Conduct 1:1 shadow sessions. Hold weekly group shadow sessions. Check in on your pod regularly.

A contributor got flagged by the system. Does that mean they did something wrong?

Not necessarily. A flag is a signal to investigate, not an automatic verdict. You decide what it means after looking into it.

How do I spot, and handle, AI-written justifications?

Common tells:

  • Hallucinations: referencing people or objects that aren't in the image, oddly precise measurements or color names, missing an obvious issue while fixating on something trivial.
  • Storytelling: inventing emotions or narratives the task didn't ask for ('the woman is preparing for a journey').
  • Writing that's too perfect: no typos, unusually formal vocabulary, sudden shifts to bulleted lists, heavy use of semicolons/em-dashes.
  • Repetition: the same stock phrases ('upon closer inspection,' 'overall') repeated across many tasks, or the prompt echoed back verbatim.
  • Behavioral signs: sudden metric shifts, unfamiliarity with their own work in a 1:1, suspicious efficiency for someone new, minimal community engagement.
If you're seeing these signs, here's how to handle it:
  1. Talk to the contributor directly first. There's often a reasonable explanation.
  2. Do research: compare early tasks to recent ones, read justifications out loud, check for a resume/experience mismatch, shadow them on a task.
  3. Take notes or screenshots. If a contributor is offboarded, you lose access to their tasks.
  4. Track what's discussed in meetings so you can reference prior warnings or remediation attempts.

How am I evaluated as a pod lead?

The rubric is built around four criteria:

  • Activation: Help identify who's active on the platform and propose activation initiatives for contributors who've onboarded but haven't started tasking, or have gone inactive. In practice: review onboarding, offer 1:1s/shadow sessions/pointed feedback, and flag when your pod can take on more contributors.
  • Engagement: Spot gaps in contributor understanding that are driving quality issues, build initiatives to close them, and escalate quality questions before they become problems. In practice: review contributors with high idle time, build strategies to reduce unproductive hours, and escalate anything eating into tasking time.
  • Quality: Review productive hours on the platform and identify anything keeping a contributor from working efficiently. In practice: review low-quality tasks and send pointed feedback, compile common errors and share them with the pod, and escalate quality questions to Ops.
  • Communication & Community: Be a key part of communication with contributors to drive retention and engagement, and make sure they know what resources are available to them. In practice: communicate with your pod consistently and be their point of contact.

What's the visibility into QA scores, and how can a few audited tasks be representative?

MM shares feedback samples with us only occasionally, and we're exploring turning them into a library you can browse (though it likely won't be specific to your own contributors, given the small volume). Their QA workforce is limited and allocated by client priority, so reviews come and go. Because of that, it's on us to set contributors up well before they go into MM, so keep reviewing their image and video calibrations, and we'll start sharing practice tasks you can run with them each week.

Contributors get a “quality reminder” message with no explanation, are there guidelines?

Clear guidelines are still in progress, we don't have full visibility into the block triggers either. What we do know is that blocking is driven by behavioral signals like speeding through tasks, taking too long, or picking the same side repeatedly, and contributors get warnings before most blocks.

What's the guideline for a contributor's productive-vs-other time ratio, and how do I monitor it?

Roughly 4 hours productive to 1 hour other (~80/20).

How do I handle a contributor I suspect of time fraud, and what happens if I report it?

Do a surprise huddle/shadow session, flag it to your Pod Lead Experience Specialist immediately, they will escalate to the fraud team for investigation.

Multimango FAQ

Calibration, signup, task types, and current quality guidance

How does Multimango calibration work?

  • Minimum 10 tasks before any pass/fail outcome, no one passes before finishing 10.
  • Maximum 20 attempts total.
  • Each task is graded pass/fail against pre-approved answers. Pass threshold is 70% of graded tasks: a pass-count metric, not an average rating.
  • The pass window runs from attempt 10 through 20, the moment cumulative pass rate hits 70%, the contributor passes automatically.
  • Milestone at 10 attempts: if not yet at 70%, they're prompted to review training and reach out to you, a checkpoint, not a failure.
  • Milestone at 20 attempts: if still under 70%, the contributor is offboarded from the project (the Failed calibration reason).
  • Pending tasks don't count toward the 10 evaluated tasks until grading completes (usually a minute or two).

What are the passing thresholds for training and calibration?

Three separate bars, don't mix them up:

  • 75%, initial training.
  • 70%, Image Calibration for Multimango.
  • 2/4, T2V (text-to-video) Calibration for Multimango.
Confirm with Ops These numbers can change. Check them against current Ops guidance before you act on a pass or fail.

A contributor failed image eval training. How do I assess whether I should pass them to calibration?

At your own discretion. Have a 1:1 with the contributor to give guidance, re-train, and have them watch you go through a task. If you feel confident they will do better, go ahead and pass them.

How do contributors sign up for Multimango?

They start from the 'Getting Started' card on their Workada dashboard, sign up at multimango.com using their workada.co email (not personal), verify with the 6-digit code, then confirm back on the Workada dashboard. Approval is manual and typically takes up to ~1 hour.

A contributor is stuck on a blank Welcome screen after signing up, what's going on?

Almost always one of two things:

  • Not approved yet: the 'Tasks' option only appears after manual approval. They must have clicked 'I created my account' on their Workada dashboard to be on the approval list.
  • Signed up with the wrong email: if they used a personal email instead of workada.co, they won't appear on the approved list and need to create a new account with the correct one.

What task types exist on Multimango, and are there known quirks?

Available types rotate unpredictably, Text to Video, Multi-Capability Model Ranking, Text To Image Compare, Reference to Image Ranking, but everyone should have at least one available at any time. Switch types rather than sitting idle if a preferred one isn't up.

Known, expected quirks, not bugs to report individually
  • 'Text To Image Compare' and 'Text-to-Image Comparison' are two different tasks despite the similar name.
  • 'No Active ELO Evaluations' message on Text to Image Compare is expected and already being tracked, just switch task types.
  • A task can time out and its type can temporarily vanish from the dashboard, refresh and try other types; it typically comes back.
  • If media won't load, refresh first; if it persists across multiple tasks, screenshot and report it.
  • Only 'Overall Preference' shown with no other dimensions is expected for certain task formats.
  • QA feedback appears at sporadic intervals, not after every task.

What's the current Multimango quality focus?

Recent, subject to change Reflects the most recent audit, not permanent policy.
Two patterns to watch for: contributors picking the same overall preference across every dimension instead of judging each independently, and missing clear instruction-following gaps on video tasks. Current guidance is to de-emphasize speed, for video tasks, that can mean watching a clip multiple times before rating.

Some contributors have several GS scores while others have none, is that random?

Yes. GS tasks are handed out randomly as contributors work, so an uneven spread across your pod is expected.

Is there a Text-to-Image tearsheet like the T2V one?

Not in that two-column format. The tearsheets we have (the contributor T2V sheet and the pod lead review sheet) are both video. The closest ops-approved content for Text-to-Image is Section 9 of the Pod Lead Compendium, the MM Six-Dimension Framework, which covers T2I scope boundaries and the common category overlaps.

Can a contributor be offboarded for a low GS score? Does task time affect it? How does the percentage change?

Yes, but the bar is low, only around 15 to 20 contributors have been offboarded for it. Task time isn't a factor. The GS score is simply correct submissions divided by total submissions.

Can contributors ask for consensus on their tasks in #contributor-questions?

It isn't something to encourage, but it isn't explicitly prohibited either.

Is there a process to escalate incorrect or unfair QA feedback?

Yes, via the Multimango Review Appeals form.

Do only Text-to-Image hours count toward the Image Active Hours on the eligibility sheet?

Yes, for taskers who haven't passed video training.

Is it okay to share MM Gating Assessment Quiz answers with other pod leads?

No, sharing answers risks leaking them to contributors and lowering task quality.

What's changing

Every contributor now starts image-only and must pass video training and calibration before picking up video tasks. You own getting your pod through calibration: you review and grade each contributor's submissions, and how quickly you do that decides how fast they graduate. There's no opt-out. New onboards stay image-only, and passing training doesn't move anyone to video-only, contributors keep getting the standard mix of image and video tasks on their dashboard.

You're grading tasks you already completed

The calibration tasks your contributors complete are the same ones you completed during your own calibration. You already have the answer key (the Pod Lead Calibration Answers guide), so grade against the answers and commentary you already have rather than building a guide from scratch.

One update since you finished: each task now includes 3 audio dimensions, overall audio preference, audio instruction following, and music/speech/sound fit (the ones most often missed on QA). Your original key covers the rest; use the pod lead review tearsheet for these three. Training now includes a few extra questions on them too.

Temporary setup Down the line, each pod will get its own distinct set of calibration tasks, and you'll build your own key for grading. You'll get plenty of lead time before that change. For now, reusing tasks you've already mastered is the fastest way to move your pods through, and it keeps calibration fair as we scale.

How grading works

  • Each contributor claims up to 7 tasks from your pod's bank and completes 3. Once they finish, they can task on multimango.com while they wait for your grades.
  • Each task compares two AI-generated video clips across the task's quality dimensions, now including the 3 audio dimensions above.
  • Review each submission in the pod lead review tool and grade it. 2 passing tasks qualify a contributor for video tasking.
  • Grade using three resources: your calibration answers, the text-to-video tearsheet, and the pod lead review tearsheet.
  • There's marginal room on close calls (for example, "strongly prefer A" may be acceptable where the key says "slightly prefer A"). Use your judgment.

Turnaround Aim to review a contributor's calibration within 24 hours as a general guideline. It isn't a hard deadline, but contributors are blocked from moving forward until you've reviewed theirs, so the sooner the better.

Time cap and pay

Contributors get 3 paid hours for video training and calibration, tracked separately on their timer. While they're in training or calibration, that cap blocks them from Multimango tasking until they finish. The timer warns them as they approach the cap, and time beyond it isn't paid.

If a contributor fails

For now, they keep working on image tasks. Failing video doesn't remove their image work.

Promoting contributors after the first wave

The first group was sent automatically (everyone marked "YES" on the eligibility list, no action needed for that run). After that, you decide who's ready. The criteria we've defined: more than 15 hours on image tasks, plus certain Gold Standard (GS) score thresholds, GS being the auto-graded benchmark tasks on multimango.com. 02The Project

What Sheets actually is

Sheets is the original TSIP project (Spreadsheets Intelligence). Contributors audit AI-generated finance work against a gold-standard answer, so the skill profile is financial modeling, not visual judgment.

The unit of work

Real financial models (multi-tab Excel workbooks) get broken into 3 to 4 tasks across every 5 to 6 tabs. Each task has an input workbook (the starting state) and an output workbook (the gold-standard end state).

The design mimics a real firm, where a senior analyst asks a junior for the next piece of analysis.

What the Contributor does

For each task, AI drafts a prompt and a rubric. Both open in an edit-and-review mode, and the Contributor's job is to fix them, not to pass them through.

The rubric is scored against the output workbook and has to reach a 100% match.

Where quality slips

The common failures you'll coach against: AI-sounding prompts and rubrics submitted without edits, factual errors left in, rubrics the gold workbook wouldn't score 100%, prompts that are over- or under-specified, and several criteria crammed into one rubric line.

1What are the metrics for each task?
